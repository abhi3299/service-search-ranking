Service Search · v11 · Ranges and type-ahead

Sort three ways, let her narrow it, and forgive the spelling

Three sorts decide the order and nothing is weighted: relevance band, then what she has booked, then what sells most. Two filter layers then let her cut the list down — treatment type, then body area — and each layer hides itself when the query already answered its question. Every position on the page has a one-sentence reason. v10.1 adds one stage in front of all of it: a word that is not in the catalogue is corrected before anything is searched, so fadial finds Aurora Facial Deluxe and waz commits to the Waxing hall.

Spelling is fixed before the search runs, not after. A vector retriever cannot rescue a misspelling — fadial is a word no embedding model has ever seen, so it has no meaningful vector and sits nowhere near Facial. Character-level mistakes need a character-level fix, so an unknown word is corrected against the catalogue's own vocabulary first, and the lift, the lexical half, the vector half, the bands and both filter rows then all run on real words. kotean fadial returns exactly what aurora facial returns, in the same order.
Three rules stop it doing damage. Only unknown words are corrected, so arms is never “fixed”. A word that is a prefix of a catalogue word is left alone, because wa and pedicur are someone still typing rather than someone who got it wrong — live search keeps working keystroke by keystroke. And ties break on how common the word is, so waz becomes wax rather than any of the other words one edit away. Distance is measured with transposition allowed, because swapping two letters — faical — is the most common typo there is and plain edit distance charges it twice.
The whole idea: type waxing and no word in it is specific enough to separate anything — so every waxing service lands in one band and best sellers lead. Type bikini wax and “bikini” separates a few services into a higher band, so they lead and popularity barely matters. Same rule, both behaviours.
Two filter layers, and each one hides when it has nothing to ask. A vague query leaves two questions open — which kind and where on the body — so it gets two rows. A precise query has already answered one of them, and a row for a question you just answered is dead furniture:

Two vocabularies, both catalogue-wide. Type is read from the name against an ordered list, first match wins, so each service gets exactly one — and order encodes priority: Verda Roll On beats its own fragment Verda, Deep Tissue beats Pain Relief, and a brand beats the mood word beside it, so Aurora Glow Bleach is Aurora and not Glow. Area is read from the name, then from what kind of service it is, then from the hall — so Aurora Clean Up and Lumen Pain Relief Massage, which never name a body part, still get one. Area covers 97% of order volume.
Where the type row refuses to appear. Threading and laser name body parts and session counts but no treatment type — three stray chips over forty results is worse than none, so a type row also requires types to describe at least 40% of what is on screen. Laser is the clean negative: its names carry Bikini, Underarms, 3 Sessions, and nothing a shopper would call a kind. Small-area waxing — Upper Lips, Chin, Underarms — genuinely ships one way, so those rows carry no type and are reachable under All.
Filters narrow, they never reorder. Both layers are applied after the sort, so every guarantee above survives untouched — a filtered list is always a subsequence of the unfiltered one. Counts cross-filter: pick Aurora and the area chips become Aurora-only counts, which is the number worth showing, but no chip in its own row shrinks because a neighbour was clicked.

Booking volume is real. Counts come from the 629,017-line booking export: 548,893 completed lines across 1,403 catalogue services, after excluding 80,123 cancelled and refunded lines. Waxing alone accounts for 335,656 of them.
Two exclusions applied. Female Disposable Combo ships with every order, so its 129,603 bookings would otherwise make it the top result for almost any beauty query — removed from popularity entirely, along with 94 other consumables and customer-invisible services. Separately, threading is filed under a sub-category named “Threading & Face Wax”, so a search for waxing matches it on filing alone. Those land in band 4 rather than leading — Eyebrows Threading is the highest-volume service in the catalogue and would otherwise top every waxing search.
Running on 2,174 live services, 456 search phrases, 214 parent–child links, 24 buckets and 548,893 completed bookings. Relevance bands, popularity ordering and family ordering are real. Shopper booking history is synthetic — real per-user history is the remaining gap. Semantic retrieval is a TF-IDF stand-in, not a pretrained model.
